# Bramley House: Care column for the new Modway rows (24 Oct 2026)

You write the Care cell for each row in your packet. Care is the `custom.care_and_notes` field on the product page. Accuracy beats everything: this text tells a customer how to clean what they bought.

## Format (match exactly)
Plain text. One block per material or component: a heading line, a newline, then the body. Blocks separated by a blank line. No bullets, no bold markers, no em dashes or en dashes anywhere, no semicolons used as dashes. Example of the shape (an earlier Modway row):

Upholstery
The linen-blend fabric carries cleaning code W, which means water-based cleaning only. Vacuum regularly, or brush lightly with a soft non-metallic brush, to lift dust before it settles in. Blot spills straight away, working from the outside of the mark in toward the centre so it doesn't spread into a ring. ...

Wood base
Dust with a soft, dry cloth, and wipe occasionally with a barely-damp one, drying after. Keep out of direct sun and away from radiators to protect the wood.

A Note on Natural Variation
Where natural materials such as wood are used, slight variations in grain and colour from one piece to the next are to be expected, and give each its own character.

British spelling is fine (colour, centre); vocabulary must be American (baseboard, not skirting).

## Hard rules
1. Fabric and vegan leather care comes ONLY from `modway_fabric_care` in your packet. Restate it faithfully in plain Bramley words: keep every instruction and prohibition (cleaning code, solvent vs water, no steam, no hot-water extraction, test first, do not saturate, covers not to be removed or laundered, professional cleaning, heat and sun warnings). Do not add a cleaning method Modway does not give. Do not drop one it does give. Name the code as "cleaning code S" (or W, WS) and say in a few words what it means, using Modway's own meaning (S = water-free dry-cleaning solvent only, no water or steam; WS = upholstery shampoo, mild detergent foam or mild dry-cleaning solvent).
2. Never say or imply anything can be washed, laundered, dry-cleaned, machine-washed or removed, unless Modway's care text says so. Where Modway says casings should not be removed and laundered or dry-cleaned, say that.
3. If `modway_fabric_care` says NONE FOUND: write the upholstery block with no cleaning code and no cleaning method beyond: vacuum lightly with a brush attachment, blot spills straight away rather than rubbing, and for anything more use a professional upholstery cleaner; keep out of prolonged direct sun. Add a Review line for that row (see output) saying the cleaning code needs confirming with Modway.
4. Never claim hardware ships or does not ship. No "included strap", no "kit supplied", no "not supplied".
5. No handmade note: these are factory-made. No "A note on handmade variation".
6. A Note on Natural Variation goes last, ONLY where the materials line names a natural material: solid wood or solid hardwood, wood veneer, rattan, cane, travertine, marble (real, not faux), natural stone. Use the general wording above. Never on pieces that are only engineered wood with laminate, faux marble, resin, metal, acrylic, ceramic.
7. Heading names: plain and specific to what the piece has. Use from this set where they fit, in this order: the upholstery block first ("Velvet upholstery", "Bouclé upholstery", "Woven upholstery", "Chenille upholstery", "Fabric upholstery", "Vegan leather upholstery", "Fabric headboard" for a padded headboard panel on a wood bed), then "Wood frame" / "Wood legs" / "Wood finish" (engineered wood with a wood-grain laminate or veneer) / "Painted finish" / "Woven rattan" / "Cane" / "Faux marble top" / "Marble top" / "Travertine" / "Ceramic basin" / "Resin basin" / "Countertop" / "Metal base" or "Metal frame" or "Brass-finish hardware" / "Acrylic" / "Glass" / "Lampshade" / "Lamp base" / "Bulbs and wiring" / "Casters and gas lift", then "Stability" or "Fixing to the wall" or "Installation", then the velvet / bouclé note if any, then "A Note on Natural Variation". Never repeat a heading. Every block has a body.
8. Only include a block for a material the materials line or vendor description actually names. Do not invent brass, marble, glass or a finish the piece does not have.
9. Velvet pieces may close (before natural variation) with "A Note on Velvet": velvet shows soft shading or pressure marks where the pile lies in different directions, a characteristic of the fabric and part of how it catches the light. Bouclé pieces may add to the bouclé block: if a loop snags, trim it with small scissors rather than pulling it. These are general fabric facts, not cleaning claims; that is fine.
10. Stability (case goods: nightstands with drawers or doors, dressers, chests, wardrobes, file cabinets, desks with drawers or a hutch, freestanding vanity cabinets are NOT included since they are plumbed in): heading "Stability", body along the lines of: "Like any chest of drawers, this piece should be secured to the wall. Fit an anti-tip strap into a wall stud, and open one drawer at a time." Adjust "chest of drawers" to fit (wardrobe, desk, nightstand, file cabinet). Stop there. Wall-mounted pieces (wall-mount nightstands, desks, vanities): heading "Fixing to the wall", body: fix into wall studs or with anchors rated for the load, using fixings suited to your wall, and check them now and then. Do not say whether fixings are supplied.
11. Vanities: wood or engineered cabinet care should cover wiping splashes and standing water promptly, especially around the top edge and the base, and keeping the bathroom ventilated. Ceramic/resin basin: mild non-abrasive cleaner, soft cloth, no scouring pads or harsh chemicals. "Installation" block for freestanding vanities: install and connect like any vanity, ideally by a plumber; keep it brief and confident, no doubt planted. Never mention a faucet care block unless the packet says a faucet is included.
12. Lighting: Lampshade (dust with a soft brush or lint roller; fabric shades should not get wet), Lamp base by its material, and "Bulbs and wiring": switch off and let bulbs cool before cleaning; use the bulb type and maximum wattage Modway states if the vendor description gives it. No electrical certification claims.
13. Office chairs: upholstery from rule 1/3, then "Casters and gas lift" (wipe the base, keep casters free of hair and dust; the height lever should not be forced), then Metal base if named.
14. Wood care body: dust with a soft, dry cloth, wipe occasionally with a barely damp one and dry straight after, use coasters under glasses and hot dishes on tops, keep out of direct sun and away from radiators and heat vents. Engineered wood: add that standing water should be wiped up promptly. Rattan: brush or vacuum gently with a soft brush attachment, wipe with a barely damp cloth, keep away from heat and direct sun, which dry the fibres.
15. Beds: no mattress advice beyond the frame. A "Wood frame" or slat block can say to check and tighten the bolts now and then, the way you would with any bed frame. Keep it brief and positive.
16. Length: typically 60 to 200 words. Identical products in different colours or sizes should get identical care apart from the colour word if you use one (you need not use colour words at all).
17. Do not copy the vendor marketing description; care facts restated is fine.

## Output
Write ONE JSON file at the `out` path in your packet: an object keyed by row number (string): {"M": care text, "R": review note or ""}. R is only for: cleaning code not found, or a conflict you noticed (vendor text says one fabric, care card another). Self-check before writing: no "—" or "–"; no "wash", "launder", "dry-clean", "removable", "machine" unless the packet's Modway care text supports it for that exact row (note "dry-cleaning solvent" is fine when quoting code S meaning, and "should not be removed and laundered or dry-cleaned" is fine where Modway says so); every row present. Reply with only: output path and row count.
